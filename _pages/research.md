<header class="page-header">
  <p class="eyebrow">Agents and research</p>
  <h1>How I approach research with agents.</h1>
  <p class="lead">I study how agents can ask useful questions, design experiments and revise their methods as they learn. I’m particularly interested in what happens when many searches can build on each other’s work.</p>
</header>

<section class="section" aria-labelledby="directions-title">
  <div class="section-heading"><p class="eyebrow">What I work on</p><h2 id="directions-title">From more experiments<br>to better research.</h2></div>
  <div class="research-directions research-directions--two">
    <article><p class="direction-index">01 / Parallel discovery</p><h3>Many agents,<br>shared knowledge</h3><p>I’m interested in how independent searches can share tools, infrastructure and useful discoveries — without simply repeating the same work.</p></article>
    <article><p class="direction-index">02 / Self-improvement</p><h3>Improve the method,<br>as well as the candidate</h3><p>I study how agents diagnose failed experiments, revise search procedures and transfer useful discoveries to the next research task.</p></article>

<section class="section" aria-labelledby="evaluation-title">
  <div class="research-principle"><p class="eyebrow">A research principle</p><h2 id="evaluation-title">I want improvements that survive independent tests.</h2><p>I treat a promising result as a hypothesis to test again. I want to assess changes on evidence that was not used to develop them, and make the experiments clear enough for someone else to examine. That is how I want to distinguish a better research method from a fortunate outcome.</p></div>
</section>
